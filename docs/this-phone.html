This phone and away from home New — help test it

Mallard can be a Lyrion player itself, named after the phone. At home it is one of your rooms; away from home it is the only one, and Mallard switches to it on its own when you leave and back to your rooms when you return. Lyrion plays to it like any other player — your queue, your settings, volume levelling — and the phone plays the music.

Away from home: Tailscale, built in

Away, Mallard reaches the server through Tailscale, a free private network between your own devices: nothing is opened to the internet and no router ports are forwarded. It is built into both the server and Mallard, so neither needs the Tailscale app.

  1. Sign the server inIn Mallard: Settings → Away from home → Sign in to Tailscale (or set TS_AUTHKEY in the container). It joins your tailnet as musicd-lms.
  2. Sign the phone inSettings → Away from home → This phone → Sign in to Tailscale, with the same account.
  3. Leave the houseThat's all: on mobile data, Mallard finds the server through Tailscale and plays on the phone.

Only your own devices are let in: those signed in as the server's own Tailscale user. Any other device — a laptop, or the web app on a phone — uses the Tailscale app and the server's tailnet address.

The sound

  • At home, always the file itself — bit-perfect, at its own rate and depth.
  • Away, Opus 256 kbps at 24-bit/48 kHz (the default) — made from the file by the server, resampled with soxr from float, and decoded by Mallard's own libopus to 24-bit at 48 kHz, the rate Android's audio runs at. About a quarter of a CD's data.
  • Or away, Bit-perfect (untouched) — the file itself, for a USB DAC or a good connection.
  • Gapless, and Lyrion's volume levelling (ReplayGain) applied on the phone.

Now playing says which: "FLAC 24/96" for the file itself, "Opus 256 kbps · 24/48" away, with the volume levelling applied when it is on.

Choose in Settings → Audio Devices → This phone → Stream away as.

Volume

This phone's volume is the phone's own: its buttons, the lock screen, or the slider in Mallard. Lyrion keeps this player fixed at 100%, so nothing is lost before the phone.

A USB DAC

With a DAC on the phone's USB port, Settings → Away from home → USB DAC → USB direct sends the sound to it through Mallard's own USB audio driver, past Android's mixer: each sample as it is in the file, at the track's own rate. Unplugged mid-song, the music stops rather than jump to the loudspeaker.

Tracks kept ahead

Mallard keeps the next tracks of the queue on the phone, so they play through a dead spot, or on mobile data without using any: 20 on Wi-Fi and 10 on mobile data unless you choose otherwise, in a cache of 2 GB, and a track played again is already there. Settings → Audio Devices → This phone shows what it holds, and Clear.

Which tracks. Your own music files, read from the music folder mounted into the server's container (how). Qobuz and TIDAL tracks are held one ahead: Lyrion streams those to its players one track at a time, and they are not files to fetch ahead.
