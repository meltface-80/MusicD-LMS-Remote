How it works

  • Lyrion Music Server holds your library and your players, scans your music and streams it. Nothing is installed in it: the server talks to it over the network, as Lyrion's own web pages do.
  • The server (this project, in Docker) reads Lyrion's library once into an index of its own, kept between restarts, and adds what Lyrion doesn't keep: play history, favourites, album edits, merges, rescued artwork, label and Smart Picks data — all in its data volume, never in your files or in Lyrion.
  • The web app, the wall display and Mallard are its screens. They ask the server; the server asks Lyrion. Play, queue and the transport go to whichever Lyrion player you choose.
  • This phone: for each phone the server runs a Lyrion player of its own. Lyrion streams it each track, and the server hands the phone the file, or its Opus, to play; the phone says what it is playing, and Lyrion sees an ordinary player.
  • Tailscale is built into the server and Mallard: away from home they find each other over your own private network, and only your own devices get in.
