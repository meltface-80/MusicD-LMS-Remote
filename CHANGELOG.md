# Changelog

What each version of MusicD LMS Remote (the server, its web app and the wall display) and Mallard (the Android remote)
changed. Work merged into `dev` goes under `## Unreleased` until the owner says to cut a version; then that heading is
renamed to the version and the Release workflow's notes are taken from it. Lines start Added / Changed / Fixed /
Removed. Versions before v1.0.86 are described in their GitHub releases.

## Unreleased

- Fixed - This phone stayed silent, buffering, with each track's fetch ending after 15 s (owner report, both qualities): it fetched each track by its id through Lyrion's download, which never delivered, while the same track had reached the phone's player in a second. The phone now plays the bytes Lyrion sends its player (or their Opus), and plays a track by its id only when it already holds the whole track in its cache. The server's log says how many bytes Lyrion sent for each fetch. The phone's player takes Mandarin's settings for it: an 8 s read timeout, the playing track held whole ahead (up to 5 minutes or 48 MB), and 40 quick retries of a fetch that fails.
- Fixed - This phone played nothing, at home or away (owner report: "reaches 2 seconds, restarts, no audio"): the server's compression held back the phone's command channel, because Android asks for gzip by itself, so the phone never heard which track to play while Lyrion went on as though it did. The channel and the phone's tracks are no longer compressed.
- Changed - Mallard: this phone's own settings — Stream away as, the tracks cached ahead on Wi-Fi and on mobile data, the cache's size, what it holds and Clear — are on its own page, Settings → Audio Devices → This phone (owner request), not in Away from home, which now says where they went. They were and are kept on the phone, each phone its own. The page no longer says Lyrion knows it as “” when Lyrion has no name for it (its player runs only while chosen): it says it is named after the phone.
- Added - This phone's playback is logged end to end (owner report: "reaches 2 seconds, restarts, no audio", with nothing in the log to say why): what Lyrion tells the phone's player and what it answers, each track handed to the phone and how (by its id or as the player's bytes), each track the phone fetches with its status and time, and what the phone's own player does — buffering, playing with the format, gain and volume, ended, its errors and the fallback — sent by Mallard into the server's log (`docker logs`).
- Fixed - `/api/lms/outages` saw nothing (owner report: `"recent":[]`): the LMS client copied a fixed list of settings and left the watch out, so no command ever reached it and no outage could have been logged. It now sees every command.
- Fixed - This phone played nothing (owner report). Since the tracks became cacheable, away with Opus the server encoded a track's WHOLE Opus before sending the phone a byte, and on a small server that takes longer than the 15 s the phone waits, so every track failed and was skipped. The Opus is sent as it is made again (its first bytes at once), and kept once made. A track that won't play by its id, for any reason, now plays as the player's own stream instead of being skipped, and the reason is logged. A track kept on the phone at home no longer asks the home address for its rest away.
- Changed - Mallard: Settings → Playback and → Player settings are one page, Audio Devices, as Mandarin's (owner request). At the top the zone the screens play in; under it every Lyrion player as Mandarin's cards — its kind's icon, its name, its model, Playing / Paused / Idle / Off / Offline — and a tap opens the player's own page: On (Lyrion's power), random album radio, its name (renamed in Lyrion), About, then Lyrion's settings for it (shuffle, repeat, Don't Stop The Music, crossfade, volume levelling, volume control, on power on, sync). `/api/zones` now carries each player's model, power and connection for the cards.
- Added - When Lyrion stops answering and comes back (owner report: "Lyrion keeps restarting/crashing"), the server logs how long it was gone and what it was asking of Lyrion at the time — the commands in flight and the last ones before — and keeps the last 20 at `GET /api/lms/outages`; an answer slower than 5 s is logged too. The background read of the whole track table (dates, formats, genres) now asks one page of 500 at a time instead of three of 1,000 at once, so Lyrion, which does one thing at a time, is never held up by it.
- Added - This phone keeps the next tracks of its queue on the phone ahead of time, as Mandarin does (owner request: "add caching like Mandarin has"): 20 on Wi-Fi and 10 on mobile data unless chosen, in a cache of 2 GB unless chosen (Settings → Away from home → This phone, with what it holds now and Clear), so they play without the network — through a dead spot, or on mobile data without using any — and a track played again is already there. Each is kept in the form the phone plays where it is (the file, or away its Opus); the playing track comes first, the ones ahead wait while it is short. The server makes each track's Opus once and keeps it (2 GB, least recently used first). A streaming service's track, or one started part-way, plays as before, uncached.
- Fixed - Going away from home is smooth, as Mandarin's (owner report: on mobile data Now playing stayed on the WiiM Pro Plus, and "Can't reach MusicD LMS Remote" showed). Away, whatever room a screen was opened on, what it asks and sends goes to This phone (Mandarin's AwayRoom), so Now playing, the mini player and every button follow the phone at once with nothing chosen by hand; Now playing names it "This phone". The reach banner no longer shows while the way is changing to away, nor on one missed look (two in a row, Mandarin's rule), and says "Can't reach the Mallard server", with advice for home or away. At home the phone's Tailscale stays joined while Mallard is on screen or This phone plays, so the switch to away is at once; a Wi-Fi lost is looked at within a third of a second even when the mobile data arrive at the same moment.
- Changed - This phone works as Mandarin's phone zone (owner: "look at Mandarin and how it does it"). The phone gets ONE FILE PER TRACK, queued on its own player, never a continuous stream: the server is the phone's own Lyrion player for every choice, Lyrion sends it each track as the file, and the phone plays the file itself — at home always, and away with Bit-perfect chosen — or, away with Opus chosen (the default), Opus 256 kbps made per track from that file with Mandarin's settings (soxr to 48 kHz, from float), played at 24-bit/48 kHz. The "Lossless (FLAC 24-bit/48 kHz)" stream is gone (owner: "lossless 24/48 isn't lossless if the server downsamples from 24/192"), and with it squeezelite (no longer in the Docker image): Stream away as offers Opus 256 kbps · 24-bit/48 kHz and Bit-perfect (untouched). Lyrion's volume levelling (ReplayGain) comes with each track and is applied on the phone, as Mandarin's; with none, the samples are untouched.
- Changed - This phone's volume is the phone's own (owner: "as per Mandarin, the this phone zone is to be set fixed 100% in LMS … and volume changes take place with the phone's volume control"). The server sets every phone's player to LMS's "output level fixed at 100%" (digital volume off, level 100) in all three modes, and in Mallard every volume control on This phone — the volume buttons, the lock screen, the slider and its − / +, the Shelf's — moves the phone: Android's media volume, or the USB DAC's level under USB direct (its own hardware control in Untouched, so it stays bit-perfect). They used to go to LMS, which ignores them at fixed volume, so the buttons did nothing. Untouched no longer passes LMS's volume to the DAC.
- Added - This phone at home too (owner: "this phone zone should also be available on the same subnet as Lyrion, as Mandarin"). Mallard names itself to the server (X-Mallard-Device), which lists its own player as "This phone" among the rooms, running or not; choosing it starts the phone's player and plays here (Stream away as applies), and choosing a room stops it again.
- Fixed - Away from home, the room kept for home was overwritten with the phone's own player by the notification's service, so home again the screens came back to This phone instead of your room.
- Changed - Away from home, Opus 256 kbps is 24/48 end to end (owner: "confirm the transcoding to Opus 256kbps is 24/48"). The server already encoded from 32-bit float at 48 kHz (now pinned: `-sample_fmt flt`), but Mallard decoded with Android's own Opus decoder, which gives 16-bit. Mallard now carries Mandarin's: Media3's Opus decoder over its own libopus 1.5.2 (SHA-pinned, built with the app), which decodes to float — so the phone, and a USB DAC through USB direct, get Opus at 24-bit precision and 48 kHz.
- Added - Away from home, Untouched: bit-perfect to the phone, for a USB DAC (owner request: "it sends the stream untouched … and passes it on untouched to Mallard"). Settings → Away from home → This phone → Stream away as → Untouched. The phone's LMS player is then the server's own Slimproto player (in place of squeezelite, same player in LMS, same queue): LMS sends it each track as the file's own bytes — FLAC, WAV, ALAC, Ogg, Opus and MP3 each as themselves, at their own rate and depth up to what the DAC takes — and it hands each one to Mallard untouched, which plays them as a gapless playlist through the USB DAC and reports where it is, so LMS's progress, track changes, pause, skip and seek behave as with any player. Nothing is done to the samples (so no volume levelling in this mode). Routes: `GET /api/phone/events`, `POST /api/phone/report`, `GET /api/phone/track`; `SLIMPROTO_PORT` for an LMS off 3483.
- Added - Mallard: a USB DAC away from home (owner request: "support for lossless/bitperfect playback via usb dacs, like Mandarin"). Mandarin's own USB Audio Class driver, ported: with USB direct on (Settings → Away from home → USB DAC) the phone's stream goes to the DAC through it — UAC1 and UAC2, at the stream's own rate and the DAC's deepest depth, paced by the DAC's feedback — with Android's mixer out of the way. Allow (Android asks once), what the DAC says it takes, Fixed volume (the DAC at full), the DAC's level and its limit (the phone's volume buttons step it too), what it is fed now, and Copy diagnostics. The DAC pulled out mid-song stops the sound rather than move it to the loudspeaker. The untouched (bit-perfect) stream from the server follows; until then the lossless stream is FLAC 24-bit/48 kHz.
- Changed - Web: the search bar no longer shows permanently on a phone or a tablet (owner: "that's what the magnifying glass is for"). The bottom bar's glass opens it with the cursor in it; its ✕ clears the words, and tapped again with none closes it; a tap elsewhere on the screen closes it while it is empty. Home then has no top bar but the status bar's strip, as Mandarin's page. A desktop keeps its field in the bar.
- Added - Mallard away from home plays to the phone itself (Mallard's half of the phone's own player; owner request, as Mandarin): the moment it goes away through its own Tailscale it says hello, the server starts this phone's LMS player, and every screen shows and plays in that player alone ("This phone") — the home rooms leave the picker, and the lock screen follows it. Its sound plays from the server's stream (a background media player, PhoneStreamService, with its own notification and Stop). Home again: goodbye, the stream stops, and the screens are back in the room kept for home, which going away never overwrote. Settings → Away from home → This phone: Stream away as Opus 256 kbps or Lossless (FLAC 24-bit/48 kHz).
- Added - A phone away from home gets an LMS player of its own (server half; Mallard's half follows). On a phone's hello the server runs squeezelite for it — a real LMS player named after the phone, its MAC fixed per phone so LMS keeps its settings — so LMS plays to it losslessly and every LMS player setting applies: volume, ReplayGain levelling, crossfade, Don't Stop The Music. Its sound (to stdout, resampled to 48 kHz) is read at real time and made into one continuous Ogg stream for the phone: Opus 256 kbps, or lossless FLAC 24-bit/48 kHz. Over the tailnet, from the phone that said hello, the zone list is that player alone, as "This phone". The Docker image now installs squeezelite and ffmpeg. Routes: `POST /api/phone/hello`, `GET /api/phone/stream`, `POST /api/phone/bye`.
- Added - Mallard: Qobuz, as the web app's Browse Qobuz — a side-menu row under the plugin's own name while the server's Qobuz plugin is signed in (`/api/services`), walking its menus a level at a time (categories as rows, albums as tiles with the account favourite's ＋ / ✓), and an album's page: Play Now, Add to queue, ＋ Add to Qobuz / ✓ In Qobuz, its tracks (a tap plays that one). Search's Qobuz albums now read this server's answer (they came back with no artist or cover) and open there; their ＋ adds them to the account, where both used to say "open it from the page".
- Added - Mallard: Labels, as the web app has it — a side-menu row (while Record labels is on) to every label as a tile, its logo or its name in words, with its album count and "N merged". A tap opens its albums with Set logo (Discogs' logos, or a pasted address); a long press picks labels to merge ("Merge N into …"), and "N merged" undoes one. While the scan runs it says how far it has got; with none found, Rescan now. Settings → Labels gains the web's Label album order, Minimum albums per label and Force rescan.
- Added - Mallard: save the Library's view as a Dynamic Playlist, and edit one's rules, as the web app does. The Focus sheet's foot is Clear all · Save as Dynamic Playlist · Show albums; Save asks for a name (suggested from the rules on, as the page's) and says what it plays. A Dynamic Playlist's page gains Edit rules: its rules open in the Library's Focus with its Order and Playlist size first, and Save changes writes them back; dismissing the sheet puts your own view back. Unlike the web's Edit, it keeps the playlist's size and order rather than resetting them to 100 / Album order.
- Added - Mallard: Edit album, as the web app has it — the pencil left of Share at an album page's top right opens the same editor: Title, Artist, Year; Artwork (Find artwork lists the covers found, a tap picks one, or paste an address); Remove edits (when it carries some), Cancel, Save. Kept in the app's database; files and LMS never touched. What is saved shows at once on the page and on the walls. `/api/album` now says `edited`, so both apps know when to offer Remove edits.
- Added - Mallard: Add to playlist, as the web app has it — from the albums' multi-select (every track of the picked albums), the tracks' multi-select on an album's page, and (new in both senses) an album's ⋯. The same sheet: what is being added, "+ New playlist…", then Existing; the same toasts ("Added 12 tracks to new playlist “Road”", "existing …" when the name was taken, "(2 skipped)"), and the selection let go whatever came of it.
- Changed - The web page has Mallard's bottom bar (owner: the two apps are exact copies): Menu, Home, Library, Shelf while it is on, and Search, evenly across the foot of the screen on a phone or a tablet (never with a mouse), with the mini player lifted above it and the top bar's menu button moved into it. - Added - Mallard has its own Tailscale (owner request: "No Mallard android app tailscale login yet"), as Mandarin's phone does: Settings → Away from home → This phone signs the phone in to Tailscale (the same engine as the server's, `musicdnet/`, built into the APK for 64-bit ARM by `mallard.yml`). Away from home — the saved address not answering over the Wi-Fi, or no Wi-Fi — Mallard then reaches the server through it by itself, at the server's tailnet address, which the server says in `/api/status` and Mallard remembers; at home the engine is stopped. It uses its own local port (34390), not Mandarin's, so both apps can be away on one phone. The server answers `/api/ping`, the engine's probe.

- Added - Plugin repositories (owner request: a developer's own plugins to try). The server saves LMS's own plugin page with the list changed (LMS keeps only `http(s)://….xml`, the same rule both apps check before sending), LMS reads the repository, and its plugins appear under Available under the repository's name, ready to install.

The Qobuz label lookup through LMS's album info now asks one album at a time, 1.5 s apart, never while LMS scans, stops after five failures in a row, and remembers the albums LMS had no label for (asked again after 30 days) instead of re-asking them all on every rebuild.
